Speaker C · 2:40–4:20. The most important slide: slow down here. Read the setup line first: same model, data, steps, sequence length, batch and seed for all three rows; only the training method changes. Then walk the table left to right: trainable parameters, memory, speed, quality, file size. Point at the chart. Then compare to the paper honestly: their 10,000× is for a 175 B model where only some matrices are adapted; our model is about 350× smaller and we adapt all linear layers, so our ratio is much smaller. QLoRA was 1.28× slower per step than LoRA: the expected trade-off (4-bit weights are de-quantized on every pass). Two honest observations: (1) on a 0.5B model QLoRA uses about the same memory as LoRA, because activations dominate when the model is this small; the 4-bit saving shows on the 3B model (backup slide: full FT out of memory, QLoRA 7.6 GB). (2) 4-bit quantization alone raised the starting loss from 1.318 to 1.412, and QLoRA recovered most of that in training. LoRA ended slightly better than full FT (1.272 vs 1.285) on the same steps.
