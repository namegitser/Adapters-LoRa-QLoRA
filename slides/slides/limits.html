Honest weakness: Biderman et al. 2024, “LoRA Learns Less and Forgets Less”. On code and math LoRA lags full fine-tuning, but keeps more of the base model’s skills. Ours: WikiText-2 perplexity +1.0 % LoRA vs +2.3 % full FT.

Biderman et al., TMLR 2024 · Dettmers et al. 2023

Speaker D · 4:20–5:30. Table first: when PEFT fits and when to think twice. Then the four costs, one sentence each: new hyperparameters to tune; QLoRA trains slower than 16-bit LoRA because the 4-bit weights are de-quantized on every forward and backward pass (give our own ratio from h1_ratios.txt); 4-bit quantization can lose a little quality; adapters kept separate (to hot-swap many) and classic adapter layers add inference latency. Then the honest weakness: Biderman et al. 2024 found LoRA underperforms full fine-tuning on code and math continued pre-training, but forgets less of what the base model knew. Our H4 agrees at small scale: after the same Alpaca training, LoRA's WikiText-2 perplexity rose 1.0 %, full fine-tuning's 2.3 %. On learning, LoRA did not lag here (held-out loss 1.272 vs 1.285), which fits Biderman: the gap shows on hard domains like code and math, not on short instruction tuning.
