2 · THE IDEA

LoRA: freeze W₀, train a low-rank B·A

h = W₀x + B·A·x

  • A is r × k, B is d × r, with r ≪ d
  • Fine-tuning updates have low “intrinsic rank”, so a small r is enough (Aghajanyan 2020; Hu 2021)
  • B starts at 0, so training begins exactly at the pretrained model
  • After training, merge W = W₀ + BA: no extra inference latency

Speaker B · 0:50–1:35. Point at the diagram: input x goes through the frozen W₀ (grey) and, in parallel, through the small trainable path A then B (orange); the two outputs are added. Only A and B are trained. Work the numbers live: a 4096 × 4096 projection has 16.8 M parameters; with rank 8, LoRA adds 8 × (4096 + 4096) = 65,536, which is 0.39 %. Two design details people ask about: B is initialised to zero, so at step 0 the model is exactly the pretrained one; and after training BA can be added into W₀, so inference costs nothing extra. Why it works: Aghajanyan et al. showed fine-tuning has a low intrinsic dimension, and Hu et al. found the weight update itself is low-rank.
